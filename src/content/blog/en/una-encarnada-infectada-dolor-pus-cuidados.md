At first it is just a twinge along the edge of your big toe when you pull on your work boots. A few days later the side of the nail is red, puffy and warm, your sock rubs and hurts, and one morning a yellow drop shows up right where the nail digs into the skin. Plenty of people put up with this for weeks because "it's only a toenail." But an ingrown nail that already has pus is a small infection in your foot, and it deserves careful handling.

Here we walk through why it happens, how to tell a nail that is merely irritated from one that is infected, what you can do at home without making it worse, and when it is better to have the Clínica Hispana Mangum medical team take a look.

## What an ingrown toenail actually is

A nail grows forward, but sometimes one corner or side edge presses into the soft skin next to it. The skin reacts as if it had a splinter: it swells, turns red and hurts when you squeeze it. It happens mostly on the **big toe**, which takes the most pressure inside a shoe and the most bumps.

As long as the nail keeps digging in, the skin cannot heal. That small open wound beside the nail is where bacteria get in, and that is how the infection starts.

## The usual causes (most of them preventable)

- **Trimming the nail curved or too short.** When the corners get rounded off or the nail is cut down to the flesh, the edge that grows back runs into skin and pushes into it.
- **Tight or narrow-toed shoes.** They squeeze the skin against the nail all day long.
- **Work boots.** Steel-toe safety boots, long hours on your feet and trapped sweat are a common combination for people who work in construction, warehouses or landscaping.
- **Stubbing or crushing the toe.** Getting stepped on, dropping something heavy on your foot or kicking a ball can injure the nail and change the way it grows.
- **Constant moisture and the nail's own shape.** Some nails are naturally more curved and ingrow more easily, and damp skin softens and lets the edge break through.

## Just irritated, or already infected?

Not every ingrown nail is infected. This table helps you sort it out:

| What you notice | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Mild pain at the edge and a little redness, no pus | Nail pressing in, irritated skin | Home care and keep an eye on it |
| Swollen, warm skin that hurts when you walk | Inflammation getting worse | Home care; if it does not improve, come in |
| Yellow or white pus, a bad smell, or a lump of red tissue next to the nail | Infection | Have it checked at the clinic |
| You have diabetes or poor circulation, plus any of the above | Higher risk of complications | Get checked early, no home treatment |
| Redness spreading across the foot, red streaks moving up, or fever | Infection spreading | 911 or the emergency room |

Pain when walking is a telling sign: if you have started changing the way you step to keep weight off the toe, the inflammation is already well along.

## Home care that actually helps

If the nail is only starting to bother you and **you do not have diabetes or circulation problems**, these steps are sensible:

1. **Rest the foot in a basin of lukewarm water** a few times through the day, without leaving it in for hours. Water softens the skin, eases the pain and helps keep it clean. Skip hot water: inflamed skin burns easily.
2. **Dry it thoroughly**, including between the toes, by patting with a clean towel.
3. **Wear open or roomy footwear** while it heals: sandals, flip-flops or a shoe with plenty of space for your toes. If you have to wear boots at work, change out of them when you get home and let the foot breathe.
4. **Clean, dry cotton socks**, changed whenever they get sweaty.
5. **Keep the area clean and covered** with gauze if it rubs or oozes a little.

## What to avoid: no "bathroom surgery"

It is very tempting to grab the clippers, small scissors or even a blade and dig out the piece that is digging in. That almost always makes things worse:

- **Do not cut the corner into a point** or dig at it. That cut leaves a sharp edge deeper in, and as it grows it ingrows even more.
- **Do not push objects under the nail** or try to pry it up.
- **Do not pop the lump** or squeeze out the pus: you push the infection deeper and open the wound further.
- Do not put home remedies such as garlic, lime, onion or toothpaste on broken skin; they irritate and do not heal.
- Do not take leftover antibiotics from another illness or pills a friend hands you. Only the medical team can decide whether you need them.

## When it is time to come to the clinic

Come in to have it checked if:

- There is **pus**, a bad smell, or a lump of red, swollen tissue next to the nail.
- The pain **is not getting better** with home care, or it keeps increasing.
- The swelling grows or the redness spreads along the toe.
- The nail keeps ingrowing on the same side.
- You have **diabetes or poor circulation**. In that case, do not try to treat it at home, not even when it is just beginning: a small foot wound can get complicated quickly, so get it checked early.

## Keeping it from coming back

- **Trim nails straight across** and not too short: the corner should sit level with the tip of the toe.
- Trim them after a shower, when they are softer, with clean clippers.
- Choose shoes and boots that leave room to wiggle your toes. If you wear safety boots, try them on with your work socks.
- Dry your feet well and swap out damp socks.
- If you bang the toe, watch how the nail grows back over the following weeks.

## Signs that mean 911 or the emergency room

Call **911** or go to the emergency room if redness spreads quickly across the foot or leg, you see **red streaks moving up**, you have fever with chills, the foot turns cold, pale or bluish, or you notice confusion. Those are signs the infection is no longer limited to the nail.
