If you are a man and it burns when you pee, someone has probably told you to drink plenty of water and wait it out. In women, urinary tract infections are common and usually simple; in men it works the other way around: they are uncommon and, when they show up, there is usually a reason behind them. That is why medical guidelines treat them as "complicated" infections, and the medical team at Clínica Hispana Mangum always takes a careful look, even when the discomfort seems mild. Below we explain what that means, what the visit looks for and which signs cannot wait.

## Why men get fewer UTIs, and why that matters

A man's urethra is longer than a woman's and sits farther from the gut bacteria that cause most of these infections. On top of that, the prostate makes substances that help defend the urinary tract. That anatomy offers a fair amount of protection, especially in younger men.

The practical takeaway is simple: if bacteria managed to settle in, something cleared the way for them. It may be temporary, or it may be something that has been growing quietly for a while. Easing the burning without asking why is like mopping the floor while the faucet keeps dripping: sooner or later the puddle comes back.

## What is often behind it

This table sums up the most common underlying causes, based on general information from MedlinePlus and the CDC, and what is worth mentioning about each one:

| Possible cause | Why it makes infection easier | What to mention at the visit |
|---|---|---|
| Enlarged prostate | It narrows the urine's path and the bladder never fully empties | Whether your stream is weak, stops and starts, or you get up at night to urinate |
| Incomplete emptying | Urine that sits still is an easy place for bacteria to grow | Whether it feels like something is always left or you dribble at the end |
| Kidney or bladder stones | Bacteria hide in them and the flow slows down | Whether you ever had sharp cramping pain on one side of your back |
| Diabetes | High sugar weakens your defenses and helps bacteria multiply | Your recent sugar numbers and how your treatment is going |
| Catheter or a recent procedure | They open a direct route into the bladder | Whether you use a catheter or had something done to your urinary tract lately |

A cause does not always turn up, but looking for one changes how you are treated and, above all, how to keep the infection from returning.

## How it feels in men

The symptoms resemble any urinary infection, with a few twists of their own:

- Burning or pain when urinating, sometimes felt mostly at the tip of the penis.
- A sudden urge to go even though only a few drops come out.
- Cloudy urine with a strong smell or a pink or reddish tint.
- Discomfort low in the belly, in the groin or between the scrotum and the anus.
- A feeling of not being finished, plus dribbling at the end.

If there is also fever, a general unwell feeling or pain in the area between the legs, the prostate may be inflamed as well. That is called prostatitis and it needs its own evaluation; we mention it only because it is one of the reasons burning in a man is never brushed off.

In older men the infection sometimes barely burns and shows up instead as unusual tiredness, confusion or falls. If you look after your father or grandfather, a sudden change like that is also a reason to bring him in.

## What happens at your visit

At the [urinary infections](/en/services/infecciones-urinarias) visit you get a urine test at the clinic and, if there is an infection, you leave with your treatment the same day, although for a man the visit does not stop at the prescription, because the medical team at Clínica Hispana Mangum also asks about your stream, your nighttime bathroom trips, your blood sugar and any past stone or catheter to understand why it happened.

What the visit usually covers:

1. **Your history.** Medications you take, earlier infections, known prostate problems and whether you have diabetes.
2. **The urinalysis.** It looks for white blood cells, bacteria and blood.
3. **A urine culture if your case calls for one.** The sample is sent out so the lab can tell which germ is growing and which treatment it responds to. The result is not ready during the visit; if it means something should change, the clinic will call you, so leave a phone number you actually answer.
4. **A look at the prostate.** Through the [men's health](/en/services/salud-hombre) service the clinic checks how it is doing and orders a PSA, a blood test, when your age or symptoms call for it. Because an active infection can throw that number off, it is sometimes better to measure it once the infection has cleared. If another test is needed, that decision is made during the visit.
5. **Your blood sugar.** An infection can push diabetes out of control and, in turn, poorly controlled diabetes makes new infections easier; [chronic conditions](/en/services/condiciones-cronicas) follow-up is done here too.

## Why leftover pills in the drawer will not do

It is tempting to open the drawer and take the pills your wife had left over, or the ones from your last infection. For a man, that habit carries extra risks:

- Treatment for a complicated infection is usually different, in kind and in length, from treatment for a simple infection in a woman.
- A handful of pills can quiet the burning for a few days and hide a prostate that blocks the flow or a stone that is still there.
- Taking an antibiotic before the urine test skews the result and makes the culture far less useful.
- Every poorly chosen treatment helps bacteria become resistant, something the CDC calls a serious public health problem.

## When it is an emergency, not a visit

Some situations suggest the infection may have reached the kidney or the bloodstream, or that urine is backing up. Skip the clinic line in these cases and call 911 or head straight to an emergency room:

- You are shaking with chills and a high fever while the burning continues.
- You feel strong pain in your lower back or on one side, just under the ribs.
- Your bladder feels full, you push and nothing comes out, or only painful drops.
- You keep vomiting and cannot hold down fluids, or an older relative seems confused or very weak.

## If it comes back, dig deeper

A second infection within a few months is not bad luck. In men it almost always means the underlying cause is still there: a prostate in the way, a stone that never passed or blood sugar that is off track. Bring the approximate dates of past infections and, if you kept them, copies of earlier results. That information shapes the next step, which may include more tests or a specialist referral when results call for it.
